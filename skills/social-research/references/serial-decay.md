# Serial decay — measured numbers

Every "let's do a series" decision is a bet on episode 1. Quote these; don't invent new ones. Your own serial's
numbers go in `local/skills/social-research/`.

## Instagram 1M+ cartoon serials (five accounts where ep 1 and ep 2 are both visible)

| Ep 1 | Ep 2 | Drop |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 19M | 4.9M | −74% |
| 3.3M | 850K | −74% |
| 7.3M | 2.5M | −66% |
| 2.6M | 1M | −62% |
| 2.1M | 1.2M | −43% |

- By part 8–10 only 1–5% of part 1 is left.
- Share of the whole series carried by the first part: 35–68%.
- **A new premise resets the counter**: an account's second story opened above any sequel of its first.
- **Clones get ~7× less**: a near-copy premise two days after the original got 2.1M vs 15.9M.
- Exception: 2–3 parts a day, 16 visible parts = 34M, eleven in a row at 1.7–4.3M. Dense posting slows decay.
- The slowest-edited serial (6 cuts in 80 s) also decayed fastest (2.6M → 5K in 9 posts).
- Check the grid before computing a ratio: a missing ep 2 once produced a wrong "−75–90%".

## A brand-new game account's launch

First post ~950K views and ~85K comments; then 105K, 170K, 65K, 47K, 26K … 5–8K by day 20; comments fell ~250×.
Pilot = ~59% of all views, first 3 days = ~80%. → The value is in the launch video, not a months-long game: run a short
season with a kill rule (e.g. ≥ 50K views or ≥ 1,000 comments within 24 h = play it out).

## Reach ≠ installs

A small app's own serial can lift reach 50–100× over the account baseline while installs stay at baseline.
Check PostHog installs per day for the serial's weeks before calling it a win.

## Rule of thumb

Make several pilots, continue only the one that pops, then post sequels densely. Report the ep2/ep1 ratio
and the pilot's share whenever you recommend a series.
